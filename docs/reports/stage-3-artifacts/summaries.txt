What is procured: NHPC Limited is procuring 1200 MW of Firm & Dispatchable renewable power from ISTS-connected renewable energy projects coupled with an energy storage system, selected through tariff-based competitive bidding with an e-reverse auction. [1][2][3][4][5][6][7] Each bid may offer between 50 MW and 600 MW, quoted in multiples of 10 MW, with a lower minimum of 30 MW for projects in North-Eastern and Special Category states. [8][9][10][11][12][13][14] Storage is mandatory and the bidder must disclose the solar, wind and ESS components of its configuration. [15][16][17]

Buyer and offtaker: NHPC Limited issues the RfS and signs the Power Purchase Agreement with the selected bidders. [18][19][20][21][22][23][24] NHPC buys the power as an intermediary procurer and trading licensee and sells it on a back-to-back basis under Power Sale Agreements to Buying Entities such as Discoms and other end procurers. [25][26][27][28][29] The Buying Entities will be named later. [30]

Location: Projects may be set up anywhere in India on land identified and arranged by the developer, who must show possession of 100% of the land in its name for the full PPA term on or before the Scheduled Commencement of Supply Date. [31][32][33][34][35] The delivery point is the point at 220 kV or above at the CTU substation where the power is injected into the ISTS, with metering on the low voltage side of the CTU/STU substation. [36][37][38] The developer builds and pays for the dedicated transmission line up to the Interconnection Point, bearing all transmission costs and losses up to that point, and may change the proposed location only until Financial Closure. [39][40][41][42][43][44][45][46]

Timeline: The RfS was issued on 15 March 2024, queries were due by 26 March 2024, the pre-bid meeting was on 28 March 2024, bids are due on 12 April 2024 and technical bids open on 17 April 2024; the e-reverse auction and price bid opening dates are to be intimated separately by NHPC. [47][48][49][50][51][52][53][54][55] Bids must stay valid for 180 days, and the PPA is to be signed within a 90-day window. [56][57][58] Financial closure is due 18 months and scheduled commissioning 24 months from the effective date, for a PPA tenure of 25 years. [59][60][61][62][63]

Eligibility: No prior project experience or commissioned capacity is required; the bidder need only deploy commercially established and operational solar PV, wind and ESS technologies. [64][65] Financially, the bidder needs a net worth of INR 13000000 (INR 1.3 crore) per MW, an annual turnover of INR 6380000 (INR 63.8 lakh) per MW and liquidity of INR 1276000 (INR 12.76 lakh) per MW. [66][67][68][69] Consortia may bid and parent or affiliate credentials may be used, but controlling shareholding may not change until PPA execution and at least 51% must be held in the project company until one year after the Scheduled Commencement of Supply Date. [70][71][72][73][74][75][76]

Money at risk: The Earnest Money Deposit is INR 9,28,000 per MW of solar plus INR 12,64,000 per MW of wind plus INR 14,64,000 per MW of ESS, capped at Rs. 10 Crores per project, valid for 12 months, alongside a bid processing fee of INR 300000 (INR 3 lakh) and success charges of INR 100000 (INR 1 lakh) per MW. [77][78][79][80][81][82] Before signing the PPA the selected bidder submits a Performance Bank Guarantee of INR 23,20,000 per MW of solar plus INR 31,60,000 per MW of wind plus INR 36,60,000 per MW of ESS, valid until 9 months after the Scheduled Commissioning Date. [83][84][85] An amount equal to the EMD is forfeited if the selected bidder fails to submit documents, meet eligibility or sign the PPA in time; the PBG is encashed on a per-day basis for delay in commencing supply up to 6 months beyond the scheduled date, and otherwise released after commissioning once liquidated damages are adjusted. [86][87][88][89][90][91][92][93]

Tariff or payment: The successful bidder is paid a fixed tariff in Rs/kWh, quoted in its financial bid, for the 25-year PPA term. [62][63][94][95][20][96][97][98][99] NHPC provides a monthly unconditional, revolving and irrevocable Letter of Credit sized at average monthly billing and maintains a Payment Security Fund, coverage from which costs the generator 2 paisa (Rs. 0.02)/kWh as a discount on monthly tariff payments. [100][101][102][103][104] Late payments attract a surcharge at SBI one-year MCLR plus 5% for the first month, rising by 0.5% each further month up to 3% above the base rate, and generation compensation at the tariff is payable for grid unavailability or backdown beyond 175 hours a year. [105][106][107][108][109][110][111]

Obligations and penalties: The developer must supply the contracted capacity with at least 90% availability during four daily peak hours (two within 05:00-10:00 and two within 18:00-23:00), declare an annual CUF of not less than 40% and keep it within +10% / -15% of the declared value, using ALMM-listed equipment. [112][113][114][115][116][117][118][119] Shortfall in peak-hour availability or in annual energy below the permissible CUF limit, for reasons attributable to the developer, is charged at one and a half times the tariff for the units not supplied, and continued failure is an Event of Default carrying 24 months of tariff as damages. [120][121][122][123][124][125] Delay in commencing supply is penalised through per-day PBG encashment for up to 6 months beyond the scheduled date, after which the PBG is encashed, contracted capacity is reduced to what has been commissioned and the PPA for the balance is terminated.
