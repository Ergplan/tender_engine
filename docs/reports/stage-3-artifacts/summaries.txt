The summary of nhpc-fdre-ii as stored:

What is procured: NHPC Limited is procuring 1200 MW of Firm and Dispatchable renewable power from ISTS-connected renewable energy projects coupled with an energy storage system, on an anywhere-in-India basis, under tariff-based competitive bidding with an e-reverse auction (Tranche-II, Open Category). [1][2][3][4][5][6][7] Each bidder may offer between 50 MW and 600 MW of contracted capacity, quoted in multiples of 10 MW, with a lower minimum of 30 MW for projects in North-Eastern and Special Category States. [8][9][10][11][12][13][14] Storage is mandatory, and the developer must keep at least 90% availability of contracted capacity during peak hours while declaring an annual CUF of not less than 40% and disclosing the solar, wind and ESS components. [15][16][17][18][19][20][21]

Buyer and offtaker: NHPC Limited issues the RfS and will sign the Power Purchase Agreement with each selected bidder. [22][23][24][25][26][27][28] NHPC acts as intermediary procurer and trading licensee, buying the power under the PPA and selling it on a back-to-back basis under Power Sale Agreements to Buying Entities such as Discoms and end procurers. [29][30][31][32][33] The Buying Entities will be named later. [34]

Location: Projects may be located anywhere in India provided they connect to the ISTS, on land identified and arranged by the developer, who must hold 100% of the land in its name for the full PPA term on or before the Scheduled Commencement of Supply Date. [35][36][37][38][39] The delivery point is the point at 220 kV or above at the CTU substation where the project's power is injected into the ISTS, with metering on the low voltage side of the CTU/STU substation. [40][41][42] The developer builds and pays for the dedicated transmission line up to the Interconnection Point with no reimbursement, and the proposed location given at bid submission may be changed up to Financial Closure but not afterwards. [43][44][45][46][47][48][49][50]

Timeline: The RfS was issued on 2024-03-15, queries were due by 2024-03-26, the pre-bid meeting was on 2024-03-28, bids were due by 2024-04-12 and technical bids open on 2024-04-17, while the e-reverse auction and price bid opening dates are to be intimated separately by NHPC. [51][52][53][54][55][56][57][58][59] Bids remain valid for 180 days and the PPA is to be signed within a 90-day window. [60][61][62] Financial closure is due 18 months from the effective date and scheduled commissioning 24 months from the effective date. [63][64][65]

Eligibility: No prior project experience or commissioned capacity is required; bidders need only deploy commercially established and operational solar PV, wind and ESS technologies. [66][67] Bidders must show net worth of INR 13000000 (INR 1.3 crore) per MW, turnover of INR 6380000 (INR 63.8 lakh) per MW and liquidity of INR 1276000 (INR 12.76 lakh) per MW. [68][69][70][71] Consortia may bid and parent or affiliate credentials may be used, but controlling shareholding cannot change from RfS submission until PPA execution and at least 51% must be held in the project company until 1 year after SCSD (or 1 year from COD for consortium members). [72][73][74][75][76][77][78]

Money at risk: The Earnest Money Deposit is INR 9,28,000 per MW of solar plus INR 12,64,000 per MW of wind plus INR 14,64,000 per MW of ESS, capped at Rs. 10 Crores per project, valid for 12 months, alongside a bid processing fee of INR 300000 (INR 3 lakh) and success charges of INR 100000 (INR 1 lakh) per MW. [79][80][81][82][83][84] Before signing the PPA the selected bidder must furnish a Performance Bank Guarantee of INR 23,20,000 per MW of solar plus INR 31,60,000 per MW of wind plus INR 36,60,000 per MW of ESS, valid up to 9 months after the Scheduled Commissioning Date. [85][86][87] An amount equal to the EMD is encashed if the selected bidder fails to submit documents, meet eligibility or sign the PPA in time, the PBG is encashed per day of delay in supply beyond SCSD up to 6 months and for any damages under the PPA, and otherwise the EMD is released within fifteen days of the reverse auction for unsuccessful bidders or after PBG verification and PPA signing for selected bidders, with the PBG returned after commissioning net of liquidated damages. [88][89][90][91][92][93][94][95]

Tariff or payment: The successful bidder is paid a fixed tariff under a PPA with a tenure of 25 years. [96][97][98][99] NHPC provides a monthly unconditional, revolving and irrevocable Letter of Credit sized at the estimated average monthly billing, drawable if undisputed bills remain unpaid 30 days after the Due Date, and coverage from its Payment Security Fund requires the generator to pay PSM charges of 2 paisa (Rs. 0.02)/kWh as a discount on monthly tariff payments. [100][101][102][103][104] Late payments carry a surcharge at SBI one-year MCLR plus 5% for the first month, rising 0.5% each further month and capped at 3% above the base rate, and generation compensation at the PPA tariff is payable for grid unavailability or backdown beyond 175 hours a year, with 95% of exchange sale proceeds adjusted against it. [105][106][107][108][109][110][111]

Obligations and penalties: The developer must supply the contracted capacity with at least 90% availability during four daily peak hours (2 hours within 05:00-10:00 and 2 hours within 18:00-23:00 as scheduled by the Buying Entity), declare an annual CUF of not less than 40% and keep it within +10% / -15% of the declared value for the 25-year term, with 100% of the energy from RE sources, using ALMM-listed equipment and obtaining connectivity and all clearances itself. [43][44][112][113][114][115][15][16][17][116][117] A shortfall in peak-hour availability (calculated monthly) or in annual energy below the permissible lower CUF limit, attributable to the developer, costs 1.5 times the tariff for each unit not supplied, both damages applying in the same Contract Year, and continued failure is an Event of Default with damages equal to 24 months of tariff for contracted capacity. [118][119][120][121][122][123] Delay in commencement of supply beyond SCSD is penalised by per-day encashment of the PBG proportionate to the uncommissioned capacity for up to 6 months, after which the PPA is liable to termination. [124][125][92][93][94][95][126][127][128]
