## What was built

- **The comparison** (`evals/threeway.py`): per gold field, the reviewer's value from the gold record, the engine's reading in review at the reviewed version (`candidates_in_review`, what `make eval` scores) and the agent's standing finding (`accepted`, `not_found`, `withdrawn`) from the latest finished pass at that same version. Engine and agent are each scored against the reviewer with `evals.runner.score_field` — the same tolerances and coercion for both — and the pair of outcomes gives the class: `all_agree`, `engine_right_agent_wrong`, `agent_right_engine_wrong`, `both_wrong_same_way` (both miss and their values match each other by the scorer's rules), `both_wrong_differently`, `both_abstained` (the reviewer found a value, neither model did). Long-text fields are `needs_judgement`, counted apart. A gold tender without a finished pass at its reviewed version is named and left out.
- **The report** (`docs/reports/THREEWAY-REPORT.md`, rewritten on every run): counts by class with their share of the counted fields; tables by tender type, by agency and for every field with any disagreement; four lists — both wrong the same way (the schema or the document), the models disagree (a mandatory second pass in production), the agent caught what the engine missed (prompt work from the agent's evidence path, then `make eval PROMPT=<section>/vN`), both abstained (routing, a scanned page, unexpected wording); the production confidence rule with every field's instances and agreement. The rows go to `evals/results/<stamp>-threeway-<tenant>.json`.
- **The emphasis** (`evals/results/review-emphasis-<tenant>.json`, `evals.threeway.low_review_fields`): the fields that qualify, the thresholds beside them. The review API marks each field `emphasis: "full" | "low"`; the field card shows a quiet "low-review" badge for `low` (its title says the field is still the reviewer's to decide). Nothing else reads it: the approval handler, the counts and completion are as they were.
- **The operations** (API first): `POST /api/v1/admin/threeway` runs the comparison, writes the report and the emphasis file and returns the summary; `GET /api/v1/admin/threeway` returns the latest; both behind `ADMIN_TOKEN`. `make threeway` is the shell equivalent. `make agent-pass` (`scripts/agent_pass.py`) queues the pass over every gold tender at its reviewed version (`--gold`), or one tender (`--tender`, `--version`), skipping a tender that already has a pass at that version unless `--again`.
- **The eleven passes**: queued with `make agent-pass` after the Stage 5A deploy, run by the `agent` container; their turns, tokens and cost are in "The eleven passes" below.

## Open questions

- The mandatory second pass in production (the fields the report names): which model reads second, and whether it reads before publication or only before the reviewer sees the field. Yours before Stage 5C's published view ships.
- Whether to fold the agent's evidence paths into the extract prompts now (`extract/<section>/vN`, measured by `make eval`) or after the three remaining reviews add to the gold set.
- The text rule. Normalised exact match is right for scoring the engine against a reviewer who edited its words, and blind for a second model that never saw them: the 197 text, record and list rows in "engine right, agent wrong" need a judgement to tell other words from other facts. Options: score text in the comparison with the fuzzy ratio at a threshold (reported today, not counted), or a human judgement pass over the 210 rows. Your call; the report keeps the strict rule until then and says so.
- `core.identity.procurement_mode` is read as `e_reverse_auction` by both models on five tenders (four SECI, WBSEDCL) where you chose `single_stage_two_envelope` (the RfS describes both: a two-envelope submission followed by an e-reverse auction). The field's help text should say which the field means. The same for `scod_reference` (three tenders, both models read the other anchor). Schema v5 batch.
- NTPC PHES and NTPC REL: the section maps name 4 and 14 pages for the agent; worth checking whether the corpus for those two holds the full RfS or only notices.
