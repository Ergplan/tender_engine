## Domain guidance: Write the plain-English summary of a tender in eight short paragraphs, one per topic, with page evidence for each sentence.

Indian renewable-energy tenders, common ground for every section:

- Agencies name the same thing differently. SECI: RfS, Bid Information Sheet, SCSD or SCD, Buying Entity, Success Charges. NTPC and NTPC REL: IFB, NIT, Bid Data Sheet, Employer, Bid Security. NHPC: NIT, Critical Date Sheet, SCC. SJVN and state agencies: RfS or RfP, Scheduled Commissioning Date. EPC tenders speak of Employer, Contractor, Contract Performance Security.
- Amounts are often written in the Indian system: 1 lakh = 100,000; 1 crore = 10,000,000; "Rs. 9,28,000" is 928000. Give plain rupees and say in the rationale what the document printed.
- "As per NIT", "as per the portal" or "to be notified" is not a value. Return null and say which document will set it.
- If the attached document is an amendment, corrigendum or clarification, it overrides the base tender. Give a value only where this document states the new value, quote the amended text, and return null for everything it does not change.

For `plain_english_summary`, write eight short paragraphs in plain English that a reader who has not seen the tender can follow. Separate the paragraphs with a blank line. Each paragraph starts with its heading, exactly as written below, followed by a colon, and has one to three sentences. Keep the order.

1. `What is procured`: what is being bought or built, and how much (capacity in MW and MWh, or the size of the works).
2. `Buyer and offtaker`: who issues the tender and signs the contract, and who takes the power or the works (the buying entity, the end procurer or the employer), if the pages say.
3. `Location`: where projects may be located, the delivery or interconnection point, or the site of the works.
4. `Timeline`: the bid submission deadline and the other bid dates the pages print, and the commissioning, commencement of supply or completion period.
5. `Eligibility`: who may bid, with the headline technical and financial thresholds (experience, net worth, turnover, liquidity), each as the document prints it, with its basis (per MW, absolute).
6. `Money at risk`: the earnest money deposit or bid security and the performance guarantee, as amounts or as the formula the document gives, and when they can be forfeited if the pages say.
7. `Tariff or payment`: how the successful bidder is paid (tariff structure, ceiling tariff, capacity charge, contract price and payment milestones), and the term of the agreement.
8. `Obligations and penalties`: the main things the successful bidder must deliver (availability, CUF, supply profile, completion) and what a shortfall or delay costs.

Evidence, sentence by sentence:

- End every sentence with a marker in square brackets, `[1]`, `[2]` and so on. The number is the place of its quote in `evidence`: the first item of `evidence` is 1. A sentence that rests on two quotes carries both markers, `[4][5]`.
- Give the quotes in `evidence` in the order of their markers. Every quote is used by at least one sentence, and every marker has its quote.
- A quote is the shortest passage that states what the sentence says, copied exactly.

When the attached pages do not state a topic, write one sentence under its heading that says so and, if the pages say it, which document will state it ("Not stated on these pages; the NIT sets the bid dates."). Such a sentence carries no marker. Do not fill a topic from general knowledge of how such tenders usually work. Give amounts as the document prints them, with the unit. Use only what the attached pages state.
