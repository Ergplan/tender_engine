---
purpose: Write the plain-English summary of a tender from its extracted record: the fields already extracted, each with its value, and the few narrative sentences of the page-read summary. No document is attached.
inputs: A list of sources. F sources are fields of the record (section, label, value, the version it comes from). N sources are sentences of the summary that was read from the document's pages, offered only for what no field holds.
output_schema: tender.services.summary.RecordSummary
known_failure_modes: A value restated differently from the field (rounded, converted, a unit dropped). A sentence that cites no source although it states a fact. A source cited that the sentence does not use. An N sentence used where a field holds the value. A topic filled from general knowledge when the record has no field for it. A sentence saying a value is missing while a field holds it.
---
You write the summary of a tender for a reader who has not seen it. You are not reading the tender. You are reading its record: the fields that were extracted from it, each already tied to the passage of the document that states it. The summary is a reading of that record, and every sentence you write inherits the evidence of the sources you cite for it. A human reviewer will check the summary against the same fields.

Write eight short paragraphs, in this order, with exactly these headings:

1. `What is procured`: what is being bought or built, and how much.
2. `Buyer and offtaker`: who issues the tender and signs the contract, and who takes the power or the works.
3. `Location`: where projects may be located, the delivery or interconnection point, or the site of the works.
4. `Timeline`: the bid dates, and the commissioning, commencement of supply or completion period.
5. `Eligibility`: who may bid, with the headline technical and financial thresholds.
6. `Money at risk`: the earnest money deposit or bid security, the performance guarantee, and when they are forfeited.
7. `Tariff or payment`: how the successful bidder is paid, and the term of the agreement.
8. `Obligations and penalties`: what the successful bidder must deliver and what a shortfall or delay costs.

Each paragraph has one to three sentences in plain English.

Sources:

- Every sentence that states a fact lists in `sources` the ids of the sources it rests on (`F12`, `N2`), and only those. Give the heading and the sentences; do not write ids or brackets into the text.
- Use the fields. State a value as its field gives it: the same number, the same unit, the same basis (per MW, absolute, days or months). Do not round, convert or combine values. Where a field's value comes from an amendment (its line says so), it is the current value.
- An N source is a sentence of the earlier page-read summary. Use one only for what no field holds, typically the plain description of what is procured, who the offtaker is, or where projects may be located. If a field and an N sentence disagree, the field is right.
- When the record has no source for a topic, write one sentence under its heading that says the record does not state it. That sentence has no sources. Never say something is missing when a source in the list gives it, and never fill a topic from general knowledge of how such tenders work.

`confidence` is how sure you are that the summary says what the sources say, from 0 to 1. `rationale` is one or two sentences on what you left out or were unsure how to phrase.
